Big idea: CRWV rent super-computer chips (Nvidia) to AI tribes. Tribe pay many years. Demand huge. But CRWV borrow giant pile of money to buy chips, and now money-lenders get scared. That why rock fall 11.9% on Oct 16, down about 25% in one week.

Executive summary

Verdict: Speculative / high-risk. Respect the business, avoid sizing it like a normal stock, and don't try to catch the falling knife before the Q3 report (expected Nov 11, 2026) and a calmer credit market. CoreWeave has real demand: revenue more than doubled to $2.575B in Q2 2026, and the company reports a backlog of about $104B with more than $25B of new commitments since. But it is also a heavily indebted, loss-making company that borrows at about 9–10% (and now, in the bond market, at 15%+) to buy Nvidia chips and build data centers, and it burns billions of dollars a quarter. The Oct 14–16 selloff was the market pricing that financing risk. Equity here is a leveraged bet that demand, lenders and construction schedules all keep cooperating. The founders have also sold well over $2B of stock in the past year.

  • Growth is real. Q2 2026 revenue was $2,575M, up 112% from $1,212M a year earlier (Q2 earnings release, Aug 11, 2026). FY2025 revenue was $5,131M, up from $1,915M in FY2024 and $229M in FY2023 (10-K XBRL, financials DB). 2026 revenue guidance is $12.4–13.2B (Q2 call, per Constellation Research).
  • It loses money, and interest is the reason. Q2 2026 operating loss was $49M, but net interest expense was $640M and the net loss was $626M. Q3 interest is guided at $860–950M (CFO, Q2 call, per Constellation Research). Adjusted EBITDA margin is 59%, but adjusted operating income was only $128M (5% margin), down from 16% a year earlier. Depreciation was $1,393M in the quarter. LSEG says the company has never turned a profit (Reuters, Oct 16, 2026).
  • The debt is the story. Total debt principal was $35.55B at Jun 30, 2026 ($31.4B recourse, $3.7B non-recourse, net of discounts), plus about $16.5B of operating and finance lease liabilities (balance sheet; my sum of lease lines), so roughly $52B of debt-like obligations (Axios and Yahoo Finance quote about $50–52B). Cash was $5.5B (plus $1.4B restricted). Since quarter-end the company added a $4.2B convertible (Sept 2026).
  • The cash burn is huge. Q2 2026 operating cash flow was $679M against $6,422M of capital spending, so roughly –$5.7B of free cash flow (my calculation from the cash flow statement; Axios also cites –$5.7B). 2026 capex guidance is $35–39B; Q3 capex guide is $11.5–13.5B.
  • Customer concentration is easing but still high. The top three customers were 36%, 26% and 10% of Q2 2026 revenue (10-Q; not named). In FY2025 Microsoft alone was about 67% of revenue (10-K). OpenAI, Meta and Jane Street are named as significant customers or commitments.
  • The market has turned on the credit. Bonds due 2031/2032 traded near 78–79 cents on the dollar with yields of 15–16%, and five-year CDS reached a record of about 1,195 basis points on Oct 15 (Yahoo Finance, Reuters, Bloomberg via Seoul Economic Daily). The stock fell 15.5% on Oct 14, rose 5.9% on Oct 15, then fell 11.9% on Oct 16 to $61.39, about 25% lower on the week (WSJ).
  • Insiders are net sellers. Ten of 13 officers and directors sold $2.95B in the 12 months to Oct 14, 2026, 90% under 10b5-1 plans; no officer or director bought (Folio Lantern analysis of Form 4s). Magnetar, a 10%+ holder, sold $3.44B.
  • Dilution is heavy. Weighted shares went from 367M (H1 2025) to 551M (Q2 2026). New equity: Nvidia's $2B at $87.20 (Jan 2026), Jane Street's $1B, an at-the-market (ATM) program of up to 35M new shares, and converts that could add up to 52.6M shares.

One-liner: CoreWeave may be the most direct public test of whether AI infrastructure can be financed with debt. The demand evidence is strong; the cost of money and the cash burn are the weak points, and the equity sits behind about $50B of obligations.

1. Origins

  • Crypto roots: founded in 2017 as Atlantic Crypto by Michael Intrator, Brian Venturo and Brannin McBee to mine Ethereum with Nvidia GPUs. It started with one GPU on a pool table in a Wall Street office, later using Venturo's grandfather's garage in New Jersey as a data center (CNBC, Mar 30, 2025). The company changed its name to CoreWeave on Mar 4, 2019 (SEC record cited in prospectus search results).
  • Crypto winter pivot: during the 2018–19 crypto crash it bought distressed GPU hardware cheaply, grew to seven facilities, and started renting GPUs for video rendering and AI jobs (CNBC; Intrator blog 2021). By 2020 it ran a GPU cloud business, still mining Ethereum with idle GPUs until it shut the mining unit in Sept 2022 (CNBC).
  • ChatGPT moment: after OpenAI launched ChatGPT in late 2022, Microsoft needed capacity and started renting GPUs from CoreWeave, in what CNBC calls a deal worth potentially billions. Nvidia became a customer and an investor around the same time (CNBC).
  • Financing from the start: 2023 debt of $2.3B led by Blackstone and Magnetar, secured on the Nvidia GPUs, at an effective rate above 14%; over $7B more in 2024 at about 11% (CNBC citing the prospectus).
  • IPO: Nasdaq, Mar 28, 2025. The offering was scaled back and priced at $40, below the $47–55 range, with Nvidia buying shares to help; it raised about $1.5B, then the largest U.S. venture-backed tech IPO in nearly four years (CNBC). At that time CoreWeave had about 250,000 GPUs in 32 data centers (CNBC); Seoul Economic Daily now cites more than 50 data centers (secondary source).
  • Founders' background: Intrator and Venturo came from energy and carbon-credit trading (Natsource) and later a natural-gas hedge fund, not from a software or hardware company (CNBC).

2. Products

  • Core product: a GPU cloud ("The Essential Cloud for AI") that rents Nvidia GPU clusters, mostly on multi-year committed contracts. Committed contracts were 98% of revenue in Q2 2026 and H1 2026 (10-Q).
  • Software layer: SUNK (Slurm on Kubernetes), LOTA (object-storage acceleration), observability and, new in 2026: CoreWeave ARIA (an AI research agent), Sandboxes (isolated environments for reinforcement learning and agent testing), CoreWeave Interconnect (private links to other clouds, starting with Google Cloud), SUNK Anywhere and LOTA Cross-Cloud (Q2 release). These are early; no revenue is split out.
  • Hardware generations: the first bring-up and validation of Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 was announced in Q2 2026 (company), and Vera Rubin clusters were reported deployed on Oct 17, 2026 (Embers news aggregator; secondary). The company says it has maintained pricing on older Nvidia systems (CFO, Q2 call, per Constellation).
  • Customers by type (company): AI labs, hyperscalers and enterprises. Named Q2 additions include Bentley Systems, Caterpillar, Grammarly, Isomorphic Labs and Sunday Robotics; expanded: Cognition, Databricks, Hudson River Trading, Periodic Labs, Rescale and Runway ML.
  • Joint ventures and investments: Q2 cash flow shows $550M of investments related to joint ventures and $138M of strategic investments (details not verified).

3. Customers, concentration, backlog

  • Concentration over time (10-K/10-Qs; customers labeled A–D and not necessarily the same each period): largest customer 35% (FY2023), 62% (FY2024), 67% (FY2025, named in the 10-K as Microsoft), 45% (Q1 2026), 36% (Q2 2026). Q2 2026: second customer 26%, third 10%. Customers A and B were 32% and 32% of accounts receivable at Jun 30, 2026.
  • Who is who (partly inference): Runchey Research infers Customer A is Microsoft and Customer B is OpenAI or Meta; the filings do not say. Treat as unverified.
  • OpenAI: a March 2025 agreement of up to $11.9B through Oct 2030, with OpenAI receiving 8.75M shares ($350M) at the IPO; a later order form of up to about $6.5B through May 2031 (10-K and Q3 2025 10-Q via sec-api.io). Reported total OpenAI commitments are therefore about $18.4B across contracts (my sum). The infrastructure for part of this is held in a special-purpose vehicle with OpenAI holding a lien on default (sec-api.io summary of the 10-Q).
  • Meta: commitment of up to about $14.2B through Dec 2031 (10-K), plus a March 2026 order form of up to about $21.0B including an existing option (Q2 10-Q) (sec-api.io summary). Meta is expected to become a 10%+ customer.
  • Nvidia (both supplier and customer and investor): Nvidia ordered $6.3B of capacity in Sept 2025 and agreed to buy CoreWeave's unsold capacity through Apr 13, 2032 (Reuters/10-K). In Jan 2026 it invested $2B in Class A shares at $87.20 (about 22.9M shares), making it the second-largest shareholder (Reuters; SEC 8-K). Axios values the stake at about $3.3B on Oct 16. On Oct 15 Jensen Huang said Nvidia saw no need to bail out CoreWeave: "I'm optimistic. Their cost of capital is actually decreasing" (Reuters via Axios). Axios noted that falling bond prices say otherwise.
  • Jane Street: about $6.0B commitment (April 2026) and a $1B strategic equity investment (Q2 release; sec-api.io).
  • Circularity: Nvidia sells chips to CoreWeave, invests in it, buys capacity from it, and backstops unused capacity, while customers like Meta and OpenAI are themselves funded by the same AI boom. A Reuters analysis (Oct 16, 2026) calls this "circular financing" and a key worry. My view: it is not necessarily improper, but it concentrates risk in the same ecosystem.
  • Backlog quality: $104B (company definition, includes amounts it expects to recognize under committed contracts, subject to delivery and availability). That definition means backlog is only as good as CoreWeave's ability to build and deliver on time. About 40% converts in less than 24 months (Constellation). The CEO says backlog more than covers debt obligations (employee memo, Reuters Oct 16, 2026). Note that interest alone is guided at about $3.6B annualized (Q3 guide times four, my estimate) before principal.

4. Leadership quality

  • Michael Intrator: co-founder, chairman, CEO. About 16 years at carbon-credit buyer Natsource; no formal tech background (CNBC). He has been candid and combative in public, ducked a Core Scientific delay question on the Q3 2025 call (CNBC, Nov 11, 2025), and told employees on Oct 14, 2026 that markets sometimes "misunderstand and act irrationally" (Reuters). He and related entities have sold nearly $1B of stock since the IPO (Reuters via CNA; Folio Lantern: 9.65M shares for $916M in the last 12 months, about 51.8M shares still held).
  • Brian Venturo: co-founder, chief strategy officer, director; sold about $1.06B over 12 months (10.86M shares). Brannin McBee: co-founder, chief development officer; sold about $649M (7.23M shares) (Folio Lantern, Oct 14, 2026).
  • Nitin Agrawal: CFO. Others: Chen Goldberg (EVP Product and Engineering), Sachin Jain (COO), Kristen McVeety (General Counsel), Jeff Baker (principal accounting officer).
  • Insider selling in total: $2.95B by officers and directors; about 89% by the three co-founders; 90% under 10b5-1 plans; director Jack Cogen sold $214M in May 2026 with no plan disclosed; no insider open-market purchases (Folio Lantern). Bloomberg counts $2.4B sold by the three co-founders in 2026 so far (via Seoul Economic Daily). The numbers differ because of different time windows.
  • Governance (not fully verified): multi-class share structure (Class A, B, C); the founders and Magnetar are large holders. I did not verify board independence, executive pay or voting-power figures.
  • Track record: the founders built a business from a pool table to 1.5 GW of active power in under a decade, survived a weak IPO and several financing squeezes, and have repeatedly closed large contracts. That is real. Offsetting: fast insider selling, aggressive debt, and a pattern of rosy messaging.

5. Passion vs "song and dance"

Real substance:

  • Reported revenue more than doubled for five-plus quarters; Q2 2026 beat expectations on EPS (–$1.03 per the aggregator vs –$1.23 expected) and the stock rose about 19% the next day (FXEmpire).
  • Contracts with Meta, OpenAI, Microsoft, Nvidia and Jane Street are public and sized in filings.
  • Raised more than $10B of unsecured debt and converts in H1 2026, including a Eurobond, plus a $3.1B syndicated delayed-draw term loan (Q2 release): proof that capital markets were open, until October.
  • First to bring up Vera Rubin NVL72 (company claim).
  • The CEO's point that this company does not buy chips speculatively, only against signed contracts, was echoed by a Moody's analyst (Reuters, Oct 16, 2026).

Song-and-dance signals:

  • "Adjusted" metrics. The 59% adjusted EBITDA margin excludes about $1.4B a quarter of depreciation and $0.6B of interest. These costs are real for a business that must keep replacing hardware.
  • Backlog is not cash. The $104B figure includes amounts "we estimate will be recognized," subject to delivery. The headline numbers come with big capital needs.
  • "Inflection point" language. Q2 release says scale is "beginning to translate into operating leverage," yet adjusted operating margin fell to 5% from 16% (the company's own tables).
  • Insiders sell while promoting. Heavy founder sales via plans: legal and common, but not a vote of confidence at this scale. The memo says the market is irrational while the founders sold about $2.4–2.6B this year.
  • Nvidia comfort. The "cost of capital is decreasing" quote is contradicted by 15–16% bond yields at the same time (Axios).
  • Quarterly "who is this customer?" Customers are lettered; sizes change; named relationships are less clear than the press releases suggest.

Call: operational passion and delivery look real; the financial narrative is the part that needs the skepticism.

  • June 2024: CoreWeave's all-cash offer was rejected (Reuters).
  • July 7, 2025: CoreWeave agreed to an all-stock deal valued at about $9B (Reuters; 8-K).
  • Oct 30, 2025: Core Scientific shareholders voted it down: 203,451,498 against vs 20,752,327 for, 21,588,639 abstaining; the agreement terminated that day. Two Seas Capital, which says it is the largest active CORZ holder, campaigned against it, citing the fixed exchange ratio and valuation (Reuters; CORZ 8-K).
  • Aftermath: the commercial relationship continues. CoreWeave said it would keep pursuing "opportunistic and strategic M&A." The deal's failure meant CoreWeave kept paying rent to a third party instead of owning the sites, which matters for margins and delivery risk. A few weeks later, delays at a third-party developer (reported as Core Scientific) cut CoreWeave's guidance (see Section 6).

10. Competition

  • Hyperscalers (Microsoft, AWS, Google, Oracle): they are both customers/partners (Microsoft was 67% of FY2025 revenue) and the biggest competitors. They have far more cash, investment-grade debt and in-house chips. Oracle is the scale benchmark with about $664B of RPO (BlockWest, Q2 2026) but carries its own debt concerns; Oracle fell 8.8% on Oct 14 and 4.6% on Oct 16 (Seoul Economic Daily).
  • Nebius: a Nasdaq-listed neocloud with over $40B of commitments (Microsoft and Meta deals), which looks faster-growing and less debt-dependent than CoreWeave by one comparison (BlockWest; secondary). It fell 10.1% on Oct 14 and 12.2% on Oct 16.
  • Lambda: private, preparing an IPO, about $50B backlog including a reported $35B Anthropic deal; a $14.5B pre-money valuation was reported (Plutux, Oct 7, 2026; secondary).
  • Others: Crusoe, Applied Digital, IREN, TeraWulf (Reuters, Oct 16, 2026). IREN fell 11.2% on Oct 16.
  • CoreWeave's edge: largest disclosed backlog, early lead on new Nvidia generations, software stack, and experience operating clusters at scale. Weakness: highest leverage, customer concentration, dependence on one chip supplier, rented sites.
  • Pricing risk: if GPU rental prices fall as supply expands or chips improve, older hardware could be worth less than the debt secured on it. The company says it has maintained pricing on prior-generation systems (CFO) but this is unproven through a downturn.

11. Future / catalysts

  • Revenue ramp: run-rate $18.5–19.5B exiting 2026 (guide); backlog to be converted as capacity energizes.
  • Customer mix broadening: more enterprises and labs (Caterpillar, Databricks, etc.) lowers dependence on Microsoft; Meta and OpenAI ramps.
  • Platform software: cross-cloud products and agent tools could lift margins, but no numbers yet.
  • Financing relief paths: investment-grade-like structures for contracted projects (DDTL 4.0 at 7% non-recourse shows what is possible), a backstop or equity from a strategic (Nvidia, Microsoft), or lower rates. Rate path: the Fed meets Oct 27–28 (market priced 84.5% odds of a hold on Oct 16 per CME FedWatch, via Seoul Economic Daily).
  • Index/flows: selected for the Nasdaq-100 (Q2 release).

12. Bull case

  1. Demand is extraordinary and contracted: $104B backlog plus $25B+ added; major customers include Microsoft, Meta, OpenAI, Jane Street, Nvidia.
  2. Revenue doubling: +112% in Q2 at $2.6B a quarter, with exit run-rate guidance of $18.5–19.5B.
  3. Nvidia alignment: Nvidia is a major shareholder, supplier and customer, and has agreed to buy unused capacity through April 2032.
  4. Non-recourse financing is cheaper: 7% on DDTL 4.0 against 15% on the oldest DDTL 1.0, showing contract-backed debt can get cheaper over time (10-Q).
  5. Stock is down more than 60% from its peak: much of the bad news is priced in; if credit stabilizes, the equity has big operating leverage.
  6. Execution record: the team has delivered every capacity step-up and raised guidance in Aug 2026.
  7. Distress may be overdone: Reuters notes the company does not buy chips speculatively; much of the debt is secured on contracted capacity (CEO's argument; a Moody's analyst agreed the company does not buy chips speculatively; counterparty credit quality not verified).

13. Bear case

  1. Debt and cost of capital: about $50B of debt and leases, bonds at 15–16%, CDS at record, "B" rated unsecured notes. If lenders pull back, growth stops or equity is diluted heavily.
  2. Burn: roughly –$5.7B free cash flow in Q2, $35–39B capex in 2026. Needs continuous new capital.
  3. No profits: operating loss, net loss $626M a quarter, interest rising to $860–950M a quarter.
  4. Customer concentration: top three customers about 72% of Q2 revenue; Microsoft was 67% of FY2025 revenue. The customers are themselves exposed to AI funding conditions.
  5. Circular financing: Nvidia chips, investment, capacity backstop and customers' purchases are intertwined.
  6. Delivery risk: third-party sites; 2025 delay; backlog is subject to delivery.
  7. Technology and price risk: GPU obsolescence and rental price declines versus long-dated debt.
  8. Insider selling: $2.95B sold by officers and directors in 12 months, no buying; Magnetar sold $3.44B.
  9. Dilution: share count up about 50% in a year with ATM and converts still to come.
  10. Contagion and macro: high rates and AI-bubble worries hit neoclouds first; Fed Oct 27–28.
  11. Valuation: about 10x EV/TTM revenue with no earnings; EV is more than twice the market cap.

Bottom line

CoreWeave is where the AI boom meets the bond market. The business is growing faster than almost anything its size, with a huge signed backlog and top-tier customers, and the founders built it from a pool table to about 1.5 GW. But the equity sits behind roughly $50B of debt and leases, loses $600M+ a quarter, burns over $5B of free cash a quarter, has insiders selling billions, and now faces bond yields near 15–16% and record credit-default-swap costs. The Oct 16 drop of 11.9% (about 25% for the week) was the market doubting whether the company can keep financing growth, not doubting demand. Practical stance: speculative position at most, small, and only if one can live with a large loss; wait for evidence that borrowing costs are falling, that the Q3 report shows delivery on time and interest and capex in line, and that insiders and the company are not leaning on new equity at low prices. Reasons to be more constructive: bond yields back toward 10%, a Nvidia/Microsoft/other backstop, a clean Nov 11 report, new IG-style non-recourse financing. Reasons to be more cautious: a failed or very expensive debt raise, ATM selling at low prices, backlog cancellations or delays, a big customer reducing commitments, Fed hikes.
