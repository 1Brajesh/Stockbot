🪨Caveman version

The whole memo in plain words. Full detail follows below.

Big idea: DDOG make dashboard tribe use to watch if their computer stuff break. 33,400 tribes pay. Business very good, money coming fast. But rock price already high, and one big AI tribe (maybe OpenAI) start using less. Good rock, careful with price. No chase.

Where come from: Two French-born friends, Olivier and Alexis, work at same NYC school-software firm in 2010. Dev tribe and ops tribe always fight. They build tool so both see same thing. Still run company after 16 years. Not hired CEO. Founder.

What sell: One big toolbox. Watch servers, apps, logs, user clicks, and security. New: watch AI robots and the chips they run on (GPU). Also "Bits AI" helper robot that find broken thing. Most customers buy many tools. 58% buy 4 or more.

Real: Sales $1.12 billion in one quarter, up 36%. Last year $3.43 billion, up 28%. Customers keep paying and spend more (about 120% of last year). Almost nobody leave. Real cash: about $1.07 billion free cash in last 12 months. $5 billion in bank. Little debt ($1B, no interest).

Dance and worry:

  • Biggest customer (company not say name, analysts say OpenAI) cut usage starting July. Datadog say this could slow growth. Next quarter growth guided 28-29%, down from 36%.
  • "Profit" two ways. Big-word profit 23%. Real accounting profit only about 0.5%. Difference is paying workers with stock, about one-fifth of sales.
  • Share count creeps up 2-3% a year. No buyback found.
  • Founders sell some stock on schedule. Normal, but watch. Founders also hold extra votes (about 42%). Company also move legal home to Nevada in April 2026. Reason not checked.
  • AI story told in words more than dollars.

Rivals: Dynatrace, Cisco/Splunk, Grafana (free open tool), Microsoft, Amazon, Google, and AI tribes that might build own tool. Some customers grumble that bill hard to predict.

Price: $247. Was $98 in February, top $300 on Oct 12, fell 7% on Oct 16 for no clear reason. Whole tribe worth about $89B. That is about 21 times yearly sales and about 80 times yearly cash. Expensive. Wall Street say "buy," average target about $290. Targets chase price.

Watch: Nov 4, 2026, before market open: is growth still near 30% without the big AI tribe? Does stock pay shrink? Any buyback? Does gross margin hold near 80%?

Final grunt: Best-run tribe in its field, strong cash, honest talk about the risk. But price already pay for a lot. Wait for pullback or clean Nov 4 report. No chase. Not money advice.

Executive summary

Verdict: Quality Hold / don't chase at this price. Datadog is the best-run company in the observability category. The founders still run it 16 years in, revenue is growing 30%+ at $4B scale, it makes real free cash, and the product keeps broadening. Waiting for a better entry is reasonable: the stock has already rallied about 150% from its Feb 2026 low, it trades at roughly 80x trailing free cash flow, and one AI customer (widely believed to be OpenAI) has started cutting usage. A better entry would come after the Q3 report (Nov 4, 2026) shows that growth excluding that customer is holding up, or after a price pullback toward a more normal multiple. The business is excellent; the question is the price.

  • Growth is real and re-accelerating. Q2 2026 revenue was $1.121B, up 36% (10-Q). Growth excluding AI-native customers was "high 20s" percent and accelerating for five quarters, per management. FY2025 revenue was $3.43B, up 28% (10-K).

One-liner: Datadog is a rare founder-led software compounder with real growth and real cash, whose stock has already priced in a lot of AI upside. The thesis is intact; the margin of safety is thin, and one AI customer is the near-term swing factor.

1. Origins

  • Founders: Olivier Pomel and Alexis Lê-Quôc, both educated in Paris, met at Wireless Generation, a New York education-software company, where Pomel ran software development and Lê-Quôc ran IT operations (Crain's New York). They had known each other since university (TechCrunch, Apr 21, 2018). Their idea was to bridge the developer and operations cultures, which they saw clashing.
  • Incorporation: June 4, 2010, in Delaware (10-Q). Cloud monitoring launched in general availability in 2012 as companies moved to AWS (TechCrunch; Crain's).
  • Early scale: "north of $100 million" of recurring revenue and "doubling every year" by 2018, with 600+ employees and about $150M of venture funding (TechCrunch, Apr 21, 2018).
  • IPO: Sept 2019 on Nasdaq; Crain's described it as the biggest venture-backed tech IPO in New York history, raising roughly $700M. I did not verify the exact IPO date or price in this research.
  • Revenue path (10-K XBRL): $101M (2017), $198M (2018), $363M (2019), $603M (2020), $1.03B (2021), $1.68B (2022), $2.13B (2023), $2.68B (2024), $3.43B (2025).
  • Reincorporation: on April 21, 2026 Datadog converted from a Delaware corporation to a Nevada corporation (10-Q). I did not find or verify the company's stated reason. Nevada law is generally seen as more management-friendly; treat that as my interpretation, not a company statement.
  • Feb 2026 crash: the stock fell to $98 on Feb 24, 2026 during a broad software selloff driven by fears that AI agents (Anthropic, OpenAI) would erode SaaS demand, including a dip after Anthropic's Claude Code Security launch (Reuters, Feb 2026; Simply Wall St). Analysts called it largely narrative-driven. It then recovered about 150% by Oct 2026.

2. Products

Datadog sells a single cloud platform with usage-based modules ("AI-powered observability and security platform", company wording, 10-Q).

  • Core observability: infrastructure monitoring, application performance monitoring (APM), log management, real user monitoring (RUM; now above $200M ARR and growing over 50%, per the Q2 call), network and database monitoring, synthetic testing, CI visibility, product analytics, cost management.
  • Security: Cloud SIEM, cloud security posture and vulnerability products, data security, and newer AI security products announced at DASH (June 2026): AI Guard (runtime protection for custom and coding agents), a runtime prioritization engine the company says cuts vulnerability noise by over 95%, and Bits Security Analyst. Security remains smaller than observability and faces large specialists (CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, Wiz/Google). I did not find the security ARR figure for 2026.
  • AI / LLM observability ("Datadog for AI"): LLM and agent observability, GPU monitoring, Agent Console (AI usage, cost, effectiveness), data observability, Bits Evals. Management says MCP-related volume quadrupled quarter over quarter and grew over 22x since Q4 2025 (Q2 call). These are management statements; dollar revenue from these products is not disclosed.
  • "Bits AI" (AI for Datadog): AI agents for on-call investigation, root-cause analysis, code fixes (Bits Code), release validation and test generation. Adoption is described qualitatively. No revenue number given.
  • Platform expansion: "Bring Your Own Cloud" (BYOC) logs, "infinite cardinality" metrics (a pricing change to address bill surprises), federated log search across Databricks and others, and in-house AI research (the Toto time-series model, v2 released May 2026). Adaptive ML acquisition closed June 2026 (call). Multi-product adoption (Q2 2026): 58% of customers use 4+ products (52% a year ago), 37% use 6+ (29%), 13% use 10+ (7%).
  • Recognition: named a Leader in the Gartner Magic Quadrant for Observability Platforms for the sixth year in a row (company claim, Aug 6, 2026 call).

3. Customers and net revenue retention

  • Count: about 33,400 customers (vs 31,400), about 4,720 with ARR ≥$100K (vs 3,850), generating about 91% of ARR (Q2 call). Large customers are most of the money.
  • Net revenue retention (NRR): trailing-12-month "low-120%s" at Jun 30, 2026, up from "about 120%" a year earlier. The 10-Q attributes the increase to usage growth from existing customers and warns about the largest customer. Datadog does not give a precise number, only ranges. Over the past several years NRR has been well above 100% in every reported period I saw, but I did not pull the full history (it dipped in 2023 when growth fell to 27%, per revenue-growth history in the IR deck).
  • Gross retention: "mid-to-high 90s" (call). Very sticky.
  • New customers: about 30% of year-over-year revenue growth in Q2 came from new customers (25% in Q1); 10-Q says 70% of Q2 growth was from existing customers. Enterprise new-logo bookings more than doubled year over year (CFO).
  • Customer wins cited: a Fortune 10 retailer expanding to 11 products; a South American bank consolidating on 11 products; a Fortune 100 health insurer with an eight-figure deal expanding to 19 products; an online media company with a $30M+ multiyear deal displacing four tools; two new "neolab" AI labs at seven figures each (all company-reported, unnamed).
  • Concentration: the 10-K does not disclose any named 10% customer. Datadog says its "largest customer" reduced usage from the start of Q3 2026 after renewing at nine figures (call). Bernstein estimates that customer's annual contract value at about $115M and believes it is OpenAI; Datadog does not confirm it. Another third-party estimate puts OpenAI at roughly $170M of ARR (5% of 2025 revenue); I did not verify either number.
  • Usage pricing cuts both ways: most large customers have an annual commitment plus variable usage (CFO). Commitments protect the floor but upside and downside both flow through usage.
  • Customer-side complaint: analysts note CFOs "complain a little" about Datadog bills (Goldman question, Q2 call). Pricing predictability is a recurring theme in competitor marketing (groundcover guides, 2026).

4. Leadership quality

  • Olivier Pomel, co-founder and CEO. Speaks plainly on calls, takes the hard questions on the largest customer, and made the decision to "fully derisk" guidance for that account so it would not be "an overhang" (Q2 call). Founder-CEO since 2010.
  • Alexis Lê-Quôc, co-founder and CTO.
  • David Obstler, CFO. Conservative guidance philosophy, repeated each quarter ("we imply conservatism").
  • Insider selling (SEC Form 4s): Pomel sold 89,497 Class A shares on Sep 8, 2026 for about $18.6M under a 10b5-1 plan dated Dec 15, 2025. Lê-Quôc sold 53,912 shares on Oct 7, 2026 for about $14.75M under a plan dated Jun 13, 2025 (FilingExplorer, StockTitan). These are small relative to their holdings in my understanding, but I did not pull their total holdings. Treat as routine; unverified how much they still own.
  • Dual-class control: Class B shares carry 10 votes; as of June 30, 2026 they were about 42% of voting power, including holdings by founders, directors and officers (10-Q risk factors). Founders and insiders have "considerable influence." That is a governance risk for outside holders even though it has supported long-term thinking.
  • Employees: about 8,100 in 35 countries at Dec 31, 2025 (10-K); ~44% outside the US, 34% of those in France.
  • Stock-paid M&A: a steady cadence of small acquisitions paid partly in stock (restricted shares issued for acquisitions: $75.3M in H1 2026).
  • Not checked: executive pay, board independence and tenure, any material weakness in controls (none noticed in what I read), litigation beyond ordinary course (10-Q says ordinary course only).

Leadership read: unusually strong. A stable founder team, a decade-plus execution record, and candor on the largest-customer issue. Watch the insider sales cadence, the dual-class structure, and the reason for moving the corporate charter to Nevada.

5. Passion vs "song and dance"

Real substance:

  • Revenue accelerated for several quarters, and the company showed a record quarterly revenue add ($115M in Q2) and revenue growth ex-largest-customer "pretty much the same" as reported (CEO).
  • Candid disclosure: the largest-customer reduction is in the 10-Q and the call, not buried. Guide was "fully de-risked" for that customer in Q4 (management; Bernstein notes only minimum commitment is assumed).
  • Genuine engineering output: 100+ launches at DASH, real products with measurable adoption (multi-product counts, RUM above $200M ARR).
  • Conservative guidance track record: Q2 revenue came in above the top end of guidance.
  • Real cash profits: $279M free cash flow in Q2.

Song-and-dance signals:

  • "AI is a tailwind" framing. Much AI-revenue language is qualitative: "750 AI customers," "MCP calls up 22x." Dollar contribution is not given; the 10-Q only bounds the AI-native cohort's contribution to growth at "high single digit" percentage points (7–9, per Runchey Research's reading of the 10-Q).
  • Non-GAAP vs GAAP. The headline "23% operating margin" excludes about 20% of revenue in stock comp. GAAP operating margin was 0.5% in Q2.
  • "Acceleration" is partly AI-linked. Management says growth ex-AI also accelerated (high 20s), which is a strong point, but the definition of the AI-native cohort was widened in Q2 to include hyperscalers' in-house AI labs (CFO; Runchey), which blurs comparisons.
  • Free cash flow definition. Company FCF subtracts capex and capitalized software but not stock comp.
  • Usage-based billing. "Infinite cardinality" was introduced partly because customers faced unpredictable bills (CEO acknowledged "sometimes we get it right, sometimes slightly wrong").

Call: passion looks genuine and disclosure is above average. The main storytelling risk is the AI narrative being priced faster than it is measured.

Scorecard:

7. Competition

  • Dynatrace (DT): the closest pure-play public competitor, enterprise-focused. Third-party estimate of about $2.05B ARR (Ravi Kant research, 2026; unverified). Jefferies prefers Dynatrace over Datadog on valuation (Jefferies note, Oct 2026).
  • Cisco (Splunk, AppDynamics): the 10-K names Cisco as an APM and log-management competitor. Splunk, acquired by Cisco in 2024, has a deep enterprise security and logging installed base.
  • Grafana Labs: open-source (Grafana, Loki, Tempo, Mimir) with a cloud business; third-party estimate roughly $605M ARR (Sacra/other; unverified). Grafana is not named in Datadog's 10-K, but it is the leading open-source alternative and an important price competitor.
  • Microsoft (Azure Monitor), AWS (CloudWatch), Google Cloud: the 10-K lists the hyperscalers' native tools as cloud-monitoring competitors. They are also Datadog's cloud partners and its largest sources of workloads, a classic "frenemy" relationship.
  • Others named in the 10-K: IBM, SolarWinds, New Relic, Elastic.
  • Newer pressure: ClickHouse and ClickStack (OpenTelemetry-native, storage-based pricing; ClickHouse about $250M ARR per Sacra, unverified), plus home-built tools. OpenTelemetry lowers switching costs.
  • In-house building at large AI customers: a core bear argument is that OpenAI and others are building their own observability (Guggenheim and Truist, via Woozle Research). Management says it won, in AI labs and hyperscalers, deals that previously used "homegrown technology," which cuts the other way.
  • Datadog's edge: breadth on one platform, speed of product release, ease of adoption, and data from 33,400 customers. Weaknesses: price, complexity of bills, and many peers offering good-enough cheaper options.

8. AI exposure

  • AI-native cohort: over 750 customers; 31 spend over $1M a year, 8 over $10M (Q2 call). All 10 of the top 10 AI leaders are customers (CEO). The cohort contributed "high single digit" percentage points of year-over-year growth in Q2 (10-Q). Runchey Research estimates it at 14% of Q2 revenue (third-party estimate, from the company's 2025 disclosures).
  • Largest customer: not named in filings. Analysts believe it is OpenAI. It renewed a nine-figure contract and cut usage from Q3 2026. Bernstein estimates this customer at about $115M of ACV; RBC raised its Q3 growth estimate to 34–35% (vs 28–29% guided) but built in caution about OpenAI (Yahoo Finance, Oct 12, 2026).
  • The second-largest AI customer: Bernstein thinks it is Anthropic, growing 10–15% q/q in Q3–Q4 (third-party; unverified).
  • Both sides of AI: AI raises cloud usage and creates new things to monitor (agents, GPUs, LLM calls), but AI coding and security agents could also erode parts of the software stack. The Feb 2026 selloff showed how quickly this fear can hit the stock.
  • Training vs inference: management says inference will become the dominant workload and the new "neolab" wins come from model training, which can be lumpy.
  • What's unknown: exact revenue share of OpenAI or of the AI-native group, whether Q3's reduction continues in 2027, and revenue from Bits AI or Datadog for AI products.

9. Margins and free cash flow

  • Gross margin (GAAP): 80.0% in FY2025; 78.6% in Q2 2026 (Q2 GAAP gross profit $881.3M on $1,121.5M revenue, my calculation). Non-GAAP gross margin 79.6% in Q2 (vs 80.9% a year ago), driven by AI-related infrastructure and product investments, per the CFO. Management expects "80% plus or minus."
  • Operating margin: non-GAAP 23% (Q2 2026), guided 23–24% in Q3; FY2026 non-GAAP operating income guide $1.01–1.03B, about 23%. GAAP operating income was $5.5M in Q2 and $12.8M in H1 2026, versus a $47.9M loss in H1 2025.
  • Operating expenses: Q2 non-GAAP opex grew 26% year over year, slower than revenue (36%), which is the source of margin expansion.
  • Free cash flow: Q2 2026 $278.7M (operating cash flow $315.9M minus $9.9M capex minus $27.3M capitalized software). H1 2026 $567.8M (27% margin, vs 26% in H1 2025). TTM $1.07B. Company says capex plus capitalized software will be 4–5% of FY2026 revenue.
  • Quality of FCF: FCF excludes stock comp of $750.7M (FY2025), so "FCF less SBC" was about $164M in FY2025 (my calculation). Capitalized software is rising ($61.5M in H1 2026 vs $38.0M).
  • Interest income: about $180M expected for FY2026 (CFO); that's a meaningful part of GAAP net income.
  • Balance sheet: about $5.0B cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities (Jun 30, 2026). $1.0B 0% convertible notes due Dec 1, 2029; initial strike $217.60, capped call up to $322.38 covering 4.6M shares (10-Q). Debt is small relative to cash.

10. Valuation

  • Price and size: $247.11, market cap about $88.7B, EV about $84–85B.
  • Multiples (my calculations): about 21x TTM revenue and about 19x FY2026 guided revenue (EV basis); about 83x TTM free cash flow; about 98x FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guide midpoint of $2.52. CNBC shows trailing GAAP P/E of 490 on $0.50 EPS and forward P/E of about 92.
  • Versus growth: about 30% growth and a 27% FCF margin give a "rule of 57" (growth plus FCF margin), among the best in software. That justifies a premium, but not an infinite one.
  • History: the stock was $98 on Feb 24, 2026, and $300.68 on Oct 12, 2026, a 3x swing in under eight months, mostly from sentiment (AI disruption fear then AI demand euphoria). Multiples compress fast when growth slows.
  • Street view: consensus "Strong Buy"; average target $290.38 (+17.5%); range $158–$340 (S&P Global via stockanalysis, Oct 15, 2026). Recent target raises: Mizuho $330 (Oct 15), RBC $320 (Oct 12), CIBC $315 (Oct 9), Barclays $300 (Oct 12), Jefferies $300 Hold (Oct 11). Analyst targets chase price; treat as sentiment.
  • Sensitivity (my illustration, not a forecast): if revenue compounds about 25% for 5 years from $4.46B (FY2026 guide), 2031 revenue would be about $13.6B. At 8x EV/revenue (a still-premium multiple), EV would be about $109B, or roughly +30% from today over five years before dilution (about 5% a year). The point is that, at today's multiple, the stock needs both durable growth and the multiple to stay high.

11. Dilution and stock comp

  • Stock comp: $750.7M in FY2025, about 22% of revenue (FY2025 release). Q1 2026 $196.8M, Q2 2026 $227.1M; H1 2026 $433.2M (20.4% of revenue), including $16.1M capitalized (10-Q).
  • Share count: 342.1M (Dec 31, 2024) → 352.5M (Dec 31, 2025) → 359.0M (Jun 30, 2026) (10-Q equity statements). That is about +3.0% in 2025 and +1.8% in H1 2026. Diluted weighted shares were 371.0M in Q2 2026 (vs 358.7M a year earlier); guided diluted count ~376M for FY2026, ~378M in Q3.
  • Stock-paid acquisitions: restricted shares issued for acquisitions were $75.3M in H1 2026 ($53.0M in H1 2025). Employee stock plan purchases add ~0.25M shares a quarter.
  • No buyback found: I did not find a repurchase program. Cash is accumulating instead ($5.0B), so dilution is not being offset.
  • Convertible: $1.0B 0% notes due 2029; initial strike $217.60, capped call to $322.38 (about 4.6M shares covered). Bottom line

Datadog is a high-quality, founder-led growth compounder: $4B of revenue growing 30%+, 80% gross margins, about a $1.07B TTM free cash flow, about $5B of cash, and a platform that customers adopt more of every year. The business case is strong, and the Aug 2026 results showed acceleration across the base. The stock case is harder. At about $247 it trades near 21x EV/revenue and 80–100x cash flow or earnings, up about 150% from the Feb 2026 low, after a run to a record high of $300.68 on Oct 12. GAAP profits are thin because stock comp is about a fifth of revenue, share count keeps rising 2–3% a year, and the largest AI customer is now reducing usage. Practical stance: respect the business, don't chase the price. Reasons to be more constructive: a pullback toward a lower multiple, a Q3 report showing growth ex-largest-customer holding in the high 20s or better, falling stock comp as a percentage of revenue, or a buyback. Reasons to be more cautious: a second large AI customer cutting usage, Q4 guidance below the high 20s, gross margin sliding below ~78%, or founders accelerating sales.
