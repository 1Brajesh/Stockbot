🪨Caveman version

The whole memo in plain words. Full detail follows below.

Big idea: LAC dig big lithium rock in Nevada (Thacker Pass) and make lithium powder for batteries. Rock real, big, and tribe building plant now. But plant not finish till late 2027, no money coming in till 2028, and tribe keep selling new shares to pay. Hold small rock, not big rock.

Where come from: Old lithium tribe from 2008 and 2015 merge. Fight for permit, win January 2021. GM (car tribe) put in money 2023 and own 38% of the mine now. In October 2023 tribe split in two: South America part left, USA part (LAC) kept Nevada rock.

Who pay: US government lend $2.23 billion (DOE). Then 2025 government say "we want piece too" and take 5% of tribe plus 5% of mine for small penny price. GM own 38%. Orion fund lend $250M. Tribe own 62% of mine and run it.

Real: Steel up, big smokestack stand, 1,600 workers, more coming. Spent $1.8 billion so far. 95% plans done. Nobody hurt bad. Tribe honest-ish about cost trouble. Not fake.

Dance and worry:

  • Plant cost say $2.93 billion, but tribe also say tariffs, war shipping, and inflation NOT in that number. New cost count due soon. Maybe go up.
  • Timeline slow: once talk 2026 start, now finish building late 2027, full speed 2028.
  • "Profit" this year not real profit. Just paper gains when share price drop.
  • Much cash is locked for the mine only.

Money:

  • Zero sales. Lose $86M in 2025.
  • Shares go 160M → 363M in three years. And more coming: government warrants (5%), Orion papers, and new Yorkville paper ($150M) that turn into MORE shares if price fall lower. Maybe ~500M shares if all happen.
  • Cash $1.28 billion but a lot locked. Government still has ~$1 billion more to give for build.

Lithium price: now about RMB 122,000 per ton in China, near year low. Mine plan counted $24,000 per ton. Price below plan. Demand good, supply coming too. Can swing wild.

Price: $2.35. Top $10.52 a year ago. Down about 78%. Whole tribe worth only ~$853M but own $3.5B of stuff. Wall Street say "hold," target about $5.5. Newest big bank (JPMorgan) say buy at $6.

Watch: new cost count (end of September or with Nov 13 report). Is cost still paid for? How many new shares? Plant turn on Q4 2026? Lithium price go up or down?

Final grunt: Good rock, big helpers, but tribe at bottom of food pile. If cost stay same and lithium rise, cheap. If cost jump or lithium sleep, shareholders pay first. Keep small spear. No add before new cost count. Not money advice.

Executive summary

Verdict: Speculative Hold / size small (a "could-go-to-zero or triple" slice). Don't add on construction photos or press releases. Add only after the definitive capex estimate lands (due end of Q3 2026, so likely with Q3 results) and shows the project is still fully funded, and after lithium carbonate holds well above today's levels. Lithium Americas owns 62% of Thacker Pass in Nevada, one of the largest lithium deposits in the world and, if built, the only large US lithium carbonate plant. It is genuinely being built: about $1.8B has been spent, more than 1,600 workers are on site, and engineering is more than 95% done. The problem is everything around the build:

  • It has no revenue and won't for at least another year. Mechanical completion is targeted for late 2027, with ramp-up through 2028. FY2025 net loss was $86.3M and operating cash flow was –$61.2M (10-K). The H1 2026 "profit" of $6.3M is accounting gains on financial instruments plus interest income, not earnings from a business.
  • Cost pressure is already admitted. The company says tariffs ($80–100M), Middle East shipping disruption, labor competition and inflation are "not included" in the $2.93B capex estimate. A new definitive capital estimate is due by end of Q3 2026. The market is likely worried it will be higher. That is my inference, not something the company has said.
  • Heavy and rising dilution risk. Weighted shares went from 160M (2023) to 244M (2025) to 363M now. On top of that: DOE warrants for 5% of the company, Orion convertible notes, a new $150M Yorkville convertible whose conversion price floats down with the stock, and a $250M ATM.
  • The government is now both lender and shareholder. The DOE loan was restructured in Oct 2025: the DOE deferred $184M of debt service and got penny warrants for 5% of LAC and 5% of the project JV; LAC had to add $120M to reserve accounts. That helped the project survive but gave away upside.
  • Lithium prices are below the project model. China spot battery-grade lithium carbonate was about RMB 122,350/t on Oct 9, 2026 and near yearly lows (SMM, Mysteel). The Dec 2024 technical report assumed US$24,000/t. The two aren't directly comparable (China price includes VAT, different delivery terms), but the gap is real.

The stock is down about 78% from its $10.52 high of Oct 15, 2025 and about 46% year to date. It is at a new 52-week low. Market cap is only ~$853M against $3.5B of total assets. The market is pricing in real trouble: either a cost overrun, a delay, or heavy dilution. What's left is a leveraged bet on one project's execution and the lithium price.

1. Origins

  • Western Lithium (2008): Thacker Pass began as the King's Valley lithium clay deposit in Humboldt County, Nevada, held by Western Lithium, spun out in 2008 from Western Uranium's lithium project (Wikipedia).
  • Merger (2015–16): Western Lithium merged with Lithium Americas (Cauchari-Olaroz salt-flat project in Argentina) in Sep 2015 and renamed itself Lithium Americas in March 2016.
  • Permit (Jan 15, 2021): the Bureau of Land Management issued the Record of Decision approving the Thacker Pass mine (BLM). Legal and tribal challenges followed; I did not verify their current status.
  • GM (Jan 2023): GM announced a $650M investment ($320M first tranche, $330M second tranche) with a 10-year offtake agreement for Phase 1 lithium carbonate and a right of first offer on Phase 2. Construction began in March 2023.
  • Separation (Oct 3, 2023): the old Lithium Americas split into Lithium Argentina (LAR; the South American salt flats) and the new US-focused Lithium Americas, "NewCo" (LAC), which kept Thacker Pass. So the public LAC share has only existed since Oct 2023. FY2023–25 are all the 10-K years on file.
  • DOE loan (Oct 28, 2024 close): US$2.26B ATVM loan ($1.97B principal plus $290M capitalized construction interest), announced March 2024. Interest is the long-dated Treasury rate with no spread, fixed at each advance.
  • GM joint venture (closed Dec 2024): GM took 38% of the Thacker Pass JV for $625M ($430M cash plus a $195M letter-of-credit facility). LAC owns 62% and manages the project. Press put GM's total commitment at about $945M.
  • Orion (2025): $250M strategic investment: $195M of senior unsecured convertible notes (9.875% interest, due 2030, initial conversion price $3.78), a $25M production-payment prepayment, and a conditional $30M delayed-draw note facility. Orion also gets production payments of $128/t annually for 72 quarters after first production plus 0.96% of gross revenue for the life of the mine, both applying to the first 41,500 t/yr.
  • Final investment decision, Phase 1 (2025): construction accelerated with Bechtel as engineering/procurement/construction-management contractor and a national Project Labor Agreement with the building trades unions.
  • DOE restructure (announced Sep 30, 2025; finalized Oct 7, 2025): the Trump administration's DOE reopened the loan. In return for deferring $184M of debt service from the first five repayment years to later years, it received penny ($0.01) warrants for 5% of LAC (18,268,687 shares) and a 5% non-voting stake in the JV (8,656,509,695 units), issued Jan 30, 2026, exercisable for ten years. LAC also had to add $120M to loan reserve accounts. The loan size is now described as $2.23B. GM agreed to let the JV sign third-party offtakes for volumes GM won't buy.
  • The meme-ish spike: the stock hit $10.52 on Oct 15, 2025 in the wake of the government-stake news and a rally in critical-minerals names, then fell steadily to $2.35.

Skeptical read: this is a real asset with unusually strong backing, but the corporate wrapper has had a complicated, restructured past. The share price at today's $2.35 is below Orion's $3.78 conversion price and the $4.56 fixed Yorkville price, meaning those converts are out of the money on the fixed terms; the Yorkville floating-price feature is the live dilution risk.

  • Kelvin Dushnisky, Chair. Named as Chair on the company board page and Wikipedia; I believe he is a former Barrick Gold CEO, but I did not verify his background.

  • Staff: about 94 employees (MarketBeat), most of the work being done by Bechtel and contractors. The operations-readiness team had 25 people at end-2025 and is hiring, a key gap for a greenfield plant.
  • Partners as governance: GM (38% of JV) and the DOE (lender plus warrant holder) both have strong say over major project decisions. Good for discipline; bad if their interests diverge from common shareholders.
  • Insider ownership and trading: I did not pull Form 4s or the proxy ownership table. Unknown. Wikipedia cites a 2023 news piece on insiders buying after the spin-off; not verified.
  • Compensation and controls: not reviewed in detail. No material weakness found in what I read, but I didn't read the full 10-K control section. Label: unverified.
  • Litigation: I found no securities class action against LAC. The BLM approval was challenged by tribes and environmental groups in earlier years; I did not confirm their current status. A search hit for "Hansen v. Robinson Nevada Mining" is an unrelated case (a different mine).

Leadership read: a competent project-delivery team with a very good partner list. The weak spots are the lack of operating history (nobody here has run this plant), a small corporate team leaning on Bechtel, a CFO change, and a record of returning to the market for money as costs and timelines move.

4. Passion vs "song and dance"

Real substance:

  • Steel is going up. 198-ft stack, thousands of tonnes of equipment, 1,600+ people, 24-hour construction, detailed quarterly milestone lists with numbers. This is not a PowerPoint company.
  • Candid cost language: the Q2 2026 release admits tariffs, sea-lane closures, US fabrication limits and a tight labor market are hurting costs and are not in the $2.93B. It also says a new definitive estimate is coming. That's more transparent than some peers.
  • Real backers who did their own diligence: GM (invested $625M for 38%), the DOE ($2.23B loan), Orion ($250M).
  • The 2026 capex guide ($1.3–1.6B) has not been changed since February.
  • Safety record is strong (company-reported).

Song-and-dance signals:

  • "Critical to national security" framing. CEO quotes lean on "winning the global technology race" and energy independence. The government interest is real, but the same language is used to justify raising equity and loans.
  • Capex anchor. The $2.93B figure is a Dec 2024 estimate. Management is still quoting it while telling you it no longer includes major known cost pressures. The word "continues to target" is carrying a lot of weight until the definitive estimate arrives.
  • Timeline drift. Early press had production starting around 2026 (Wikipedia citing a 2024 article); the company now says mechanical completion late 2027 and ramp-up through 2028. I can't pin down who changed what when, but the schedule is clearly later than first talked about.
  • "Fully funded" optics. Much of the $1.28B "cash" is restricted DOE money that can only be used for the project. Corporate cash is thin, which is why the company went to Yorkville for $150M.
  • Net income headlines. Q2 and H1 2026 showed a "profit". Look at the line items: gains from the falling share price on warrants and convertibles ($20.0M + $4.9M), plus interest income. If the stock rises, those turn into losses.
  • The ATM timing. Sold 13.0M shares at an average $5.36 in H1 2026 and then 1.1M at $3.87 after quarter-end. Raising capital as the stock slides is rational, but it shows who bears the cost of overruns.

Call: the project passion looks genuine and the disclosure is detailed. The financial storytelling is more cautious than hype-driven. The real warning sign is not exaggeration but the structure: every time more money is needed, existing shareholders take the dilution.

6. Financing and dilution

  • Liquidity (Jun 30, 2026): $1,279M of cash and restricted cash, of which $530M sits at the JV. Cumulative DOE advances $1,209M (third advance $342M on Jun 3). About $1.0B of the $2.23B loan is still to be drawn (my calculation), subject to draw-down conditions.
  • Remaining spend: $2.93B – $1.62B spent = ~$1.31B left on the original estimate (my calculation); the real number depends on the definitive estimate. 2026 guide alone is $1.3–1.6B including capitalized interest, of which $802M was spent in H1.
  • Equity sold: ATM 1 (2025): 26.9M shares for $100.0M gross, completed Oct 1, 2025. ATM 2 (Mar 2026, up to $250M): 13.0M shares at $5.36 average for $68.5M net by Jun 30; 1.1M more at $3.87 for $4.2M net after quarter-end. A 30-day pause on the ATM was agreed as part of the Yorkville deal (briefglance). Remaining capacity ≈ $250M less ~$73M net used (my rough calculation).
  • Yorkville debentures (Aug 13, 2026): $150M issued at 100% (plus $25M option), 5-year, 5% interest. Conversion price is the lower of $4.56 or 95% of the lowest 5-day VWAP before conversion, with a $1.63 floor (reducible but not below $0.65). Interest steps up to 7.5% (and 15% after two years) if the stock stays below the floor, if the registration lapses, or if the exchange cap is nearly used. Conversions are capped at 19.99% of shares outstanding at signing without shareholder approval (~72M shares, my calculation). A 10% premium is payable on a change of control or optional redemption. Cash repayment of principal is limited to $35M while Orion's notes are outstanding. A resale registration statement was due right after the 10-Q.
  • Rough math (my estimate): at $2.35, converting at 95% of VWAP (~$2.23) would give about 67M new shares for $150M, roughly 18% more shares. At the $1.63 floor it would be ~92M shares, but the 19.99% cap (~72M) binds first.
  • Orion notes: $195M at $3.78 initial conversion = ~51.6M shares if converted (my calculation). Out of the money at $2.35. 9.875% coupon. Production payments take a slice of every tonne sold.
  • DOE warrants: 18.27M LAC shares at $0.01 (5% as of Jan 30, 2026). The 8.66B JV units are a 5% non-voting economic interest in Thacker Pass.
  • Share count path: 160M (2023 weighted) → 201M (2024) → 244M (2025) → 363M (Aug 12, 2026). Potential adds: 18.3M DOE warrants + ~51.6M Orion + ~67M Yorkville ≈ 137M, which would make ~500M shares (my estimate, before any new ATM sales or a larger raise).
  • Debt service: the DOE loan interest is capitalized during construction. The deferral gives breathing room in years 1–5 after repayment starts, but pushes $184M later. Orion's coupon and Yorkville's interest are real cash or share costs.
  • Runway: the company says it has enough to reach production on the current plan; what I can't verify is whether that survives a definitive estimate above $2.93B plus delays. Any gap has to come from LAC's balance sheet or new capital, since the DOE loan size is fixed.

7. Lithium price outlook

  • Today (Oct 9, 2026): SMM battery-grade lithium carbonate spot RMB 119,000–125,700/t, average RMB 122,350, down 2.1% on the day. Most-traded futures (LC2701) closed RMB 117,000/t, down 3.7% (SMM). These are China prices including VAT.
  • My rough conversion to dollars: about US$16–17k/t (assumes ~RMB 7.1/USD and VAT removed; FX not checked, treat as approximate). The project's Dec 2024 model used US$24,000/t. Lithium has recovered from the 2025 lows but still trades well under that.
  • Short-term view: Mysteel and SMM describe prices as rangebound, "near yearly lows." Demand (LFP cathode and cell production, grid storage, schedules revised up, possibly a new yearly high in December) is beating expectations and inventories are being drawn down (possibly ~15 days in November). Supply growth (African spodumene arrivals, Sichuan reserves, rising output, the Jadar project's registered reserves of 2.531M t, and restart risk) caps upside.
  • Medium-term: unknowable, and the main swing factor. Plant economics depend on a long-run price. 2028 ramp-up happens in a market where new supply from China, Africa, Australia and South America has had time to respond to today's prices. Analysts differ widely; I did not find an authoritative consensus forecast.
  • What matters for LAC: the GM offtake and DOE loan reduce near-term price exposure on the build, but equity value after 2028 is very sensitive to the price. The Orion production payments also take a fixed $128/t. A rough sensitivity (my illustration only, not a forecast): each $1,000/t is $40M a year of revenue at 40,000 t, before the 62% share.

8. Competition and strategic position

  • US domestic lithium: the plant would be the only large US lithium carbonate producer by volume when it starts (DOE statement says "the only domestic source of lithium carbonate" at scale). Other US projects (e.g., Albemarle's Silver Peak, Piedmont/Sayona, ioneer, Standard Lithium/Equinor's DLE brine project) are smaller, differently timed, or different products. I did not research each project's current status.
  • Global producers: Albemarle, SQM, Ganfeng, Tianqi, Pilbara, Arcadium/Rio Tinto and a large number of Chinese converters. They set the price and can add supply quickly.
  • Edge: US-based supply, US government backing, GM offtake, a large low-grade-clay resource for decades of expansion, and the potential for tariffs/"critical minerals" policy to favor domestic output.
  • Weak points: clay-to-carbonate is a more complex chemical process than hard-rock or brine plants (it uses sulfuric acid and magnesium sulfate by-product streams); first-of-its-kind scale-up risk; very high capital intensity per tonne; a dependence on one site.

9. Future / catalysts

  • Q4 2026: energization of the site; early commissioning of utilities; peak workforce (2,000+).

10. Bull case

  1. A rare, strategic asset under construction: the largest measured lithium resource in the world (company), permitted, with a national-security tailwind.
  2. Funding in place: $1.28B cash and a DOE loan with ~$1.0B undrawn, GM and Orion as partners. Goldman cites over $4.5B of secured funding.
  3. Build is visibly on track: 95% engineering, 80% procurement, 1,600+ workers, energization on track for Q4 2026, 2026 capex guide unchanged.
  4. Cheap relative to assets: ~$853M market cap vs $3.5B of assets and a company model with NPV(8%) of $5.9–8.7B at $24k/t lithium (company model; sensitive to price). Even at a heavy haircut, the equity looks small.
  5. Lithium market is tightening: demand beating expectations, inventories falling, supply slower than hoped (Mysteel), prices possibly near the bottom.
  6. Policy support: DOE as lender and shareholder, tariff protection, and a government interest in seeing it succeed.
  7. Analyst support is growing: JPMorgan initiated at Overweight, $6 (about 2.5x the current price).

11. Bear case

  1. Pre-revenue for 1+ more years with a project that is the company. One site, one process, one commodity.
  2. Cost overrun risk is explicit: the company says tariffs, inflation and logistics aren't in the $2.93B. The definitive estimate is imminent. Any increase falls first on LAC's balance sheet.
  3. Dilution machine: shares ~2.3x since 2023; DOE warrants; Orion converts; a floating-price Yorkville convertible that gets more dilutive the lower the stock goes (floor $1.63); a live ATM. A "death spiral" dynamic is possible if the price keeps sliding.
  4. Government is a heavy senior partner: it already extracted 5% of LAC and 5% of the JV for relief. Further relief could cost more.
  5. Lithium price risk: current prices are well below the $24k/t model. A low price through 2028 hurts project returns, Orion/DOE obligations are fixed, and equity is the residual.
  6. First-of-its-kind technology risk: clay-based processing at this scale has no precedent. Commissioning delays or lower recovery would hurt.
  7. Corporate thinness: ~94 employees, no operating history, CFO turnover, and reliance on Bechtel.
  8. Fair-value accounting noise: reported profits depend on share-price moves; the "profit" headline can reverse.

Bottom line for a holder

You own about 62% of a world-class US lithium project that is genuinely being built, backed by GM, the DOE and Orion, with ~$1.3B of cash on hand. You also own the thinnest layer of a capital structure where the government, GM and convertible-note holders sit ahead of or alongside you, a company with no revenue until 2028, a cost estimate that management itself says is incomplete, and a floating-price convertible that gets worse as the stock falls. The ~78% collapse from the highs means expectations are low. Practical stance: keep it small and speculative (money you can afford to lose), don't average down before the definitive capex estimate, and judge it on three things: (1) is total Phase 1 cost still funded, (2) is dilution contained, (3) is lithium price rising toward the model's assumption. Reasons to add: definitive estimate near or below ~$3.2B with funding confirmed, stable share count, and lithium holding well above current levels. Reasons to cut: a capex estimate that exceeds known funding, a further government or lender giveaway, a schedule slip past 2027, or Yorkville conversions at the floor.
