SMCI (Super Micro Computer) — Investor Research Memo

As of market close Oct 7, 2026 ET (intraday Oct 8 ~$43.6) | Research date Oct 8, 2026 | Prepared for Brajesh | Not investment advice

Executive summary

Verdict: Hold / size as a high-beta cyclical position, not a core compounder. Don't add because of the "$60B orders" headline. Add only if the November quarter shows real conversion and clean governance. Supermicro is a real engineering and manufacturing company. FY2026 (June year-end) revenue was $39.1B, up 78%, and it is among the first to ship every new NVIDIA rack generation, with a credible direct-liquid-cooling (DLC) lead. The stock looks cheap at ~10x forward earnings and ~0.5x forward sales. It has looked cheap for years, for reasons that haven't gone away: - Thin, lumpy gross margins (6–18% by quarter, guided back to ~10.6%). - Heavy customer concentration: one customer was 28% of FY26 sales and ~63% of Dec-quarter sales. - Deeply negative free cash flow (–$7.0B in FY26). - Dilution: shares +11% YoY, plus a $4.3B mandatory convertible preferred that turns into stock in 2029. - A governance record that keeps producing incidents: the 2018 restatement and Nasdaq delisting, a 2020 SEC penalty, the rehiring of nine people who had resigned over the 2018 scandal, EY quitting in 2024, a CFO replacement promised in Dec 2024 still not made, ineffective internal controls for a third straight year, and a co-founder (rehired after 2018) indicted in March 2026 for diverting ~$2.5B of NVIDIA servers toward China.

The business is real. The question is how much of the earnings power shareholders actually keep.

One-liner: A real, fast, founder-run AI hardware builder at a "cheap" multiple. The discount is earned by thin margins, cash burn, dilution, one-customer risk, and a governance record that keeps generating headlines. Hold small, trade the cycle, and demand proof.

1. Origins

  • Founded Sep 1993 in San Jose by Charles Liang (Taiwan-born EE; ex-Chips & Technologies, Micro Center Computer), his wife Sara Liu, and Wally Liaw. Liang has been CEO and Chair since inception, about 33 years.
  • Started with server motherboards, then built a "Building Block Solutions" model. Modular, reusable subsystems let it package new Intel/AMD/NVIDIA chips into servers faster than Dell or HP. Early "green computing" angle on power efficiency.
  • IPO on Nasdaq in Mar 2007. For most of the 2010s it was a ~$2–3.5B revenue, low-margin server maker.
  • 2017–2020 accounting scandal: an audit committee probe led to restated FY2015–2017 results and late filings. Nasdaq suspended the stock in Aug 2018 and delisted it; it relisted in Jan 2020. In Aug 2020 the SEC charged the company and former CFO Howard Hideshima with widespread accounting violations (premature revenue recognition, understated expenses). The company paid $17.5M. Liang was not charged but reimbursed ~$2.1M in stock profits under SOX 304. Liaw and other executives resigned in 2018.
  • In Oct 2018, Bloomberg's "Big Hack" alleged Chinese spy chips in Supermicro boards. The company, Apple, Amazon, and US officials denied it, and no public evidence was ever produced. It counts as reputational history only, not a verified event.
  • 2023–2024 AI boom: revenue went $7.1B (FY23) → $15.0B (FY24) → $22.0B (FY25) → $39.1B (FY26). The company joined the S&P 500 in Mar 2024, and the stock peaked around $120 split-adjusted that month. It did a 10:1 split on Oct 1, 2024.
  • 2024 crisis: Hindenburg's short report (Aug 27, 2024) alleged accounting manipulation, related-party self-dealing (the Liang-family firms Ablecom and Compuware), export/sanctions evasion, and the rehiring of 2018-scandal staff. The next day the 10-K was delayed. A DOJ probe was reported, and SDNY subpoenas followed on Oct 22, 2024. EY resigned Oct 30, 2024, saying it could "no longer rely on management's and the Audit Committee's representations" and was "unwilling to be associated with the financial statements prepared by management." A delisting threat followed.
  • Recovery: BDO was hired as auditor (Nov 2024). A special committee (Cooley / Secretariat, Dec 2, 2024) found no evidence of misconduct but recommended replacing CFO Weigand, adding a CAO, a general counsel, and more. Delinquent filings were made Feb 25, 2025, and Nasdaq compliance was regained.

Skeptical read: Liang built this from scratch and survived two near-death governance events. The same company produced both events, and the 2018 cast came back.

3. Leadership quality

  • Charles Liang (68): founder, CEO, Chairman. Hands-on engineer with 23 US patents. Pay is a $1 salary through FY26 plus performance options: the 2023 award covers 5M options at $45 that vest on stock targets of $45–$110 plus revenue goals of $13–21B. The stock is sitting at the first price hurdle. He is very public on X (for example, his June 2026 post about co-building a gigawatt data center for SpaceX/xAI).
  • Sara Liu (64): co-founder, SVP, director. Liang's wife.
  • David Weigand (68): CFO since Feb 2021, Chief Compliance Officer from 2018. The Dec 2024 special committee recommended replacing him because he had "primary responsibility" for lapses in rehiring 2018 staff. The company promised to "immediately" search for a successor. About 22 months later he is still CFO and signed the FY26 10-K (Fortune, Feb 2026: "no one wants this job"). Kenneth Cheung was named CAO in Dec 2024.
  • Others: Yitai Hu (General Counsel, Mar 2025); DeAnna Luna (acting Chief Compliance Officer, 2026); Vikranth Malyala (Chief Business Officer); Tom Xiao (SVP Engineering); Matthew Thauberger (CRO, 45, newer sales leader). The worldwide sales head (Clegg) retired May 2026.
  • Board (8): Liang, Sara Liu, plus 6 independents: Judy Lin, Robert Blair (78), Scott Angel (ex-Deloitte audit partner and lead independent director, term ends Jan 2027), Sherman Tuan, Susie Giordano (Mogensen), and Tally Liu (76). The board is small, older, and long-tenured, with the CEO as Chair. Wally Liaw resigned from the board on Mar 20, 2026, after his indictment.
  • Insider ownership: Liang plus Liu hold 81.8M shares (12.2%), and all insiders 12.5%. Other large holders are Jane Street (8.6%), BlackRock (6.3%), Capital Ventures Intl (6.1%), and Vanguard entities (~10.8%). These are 10-K figures as of Jul 31, 2026.
  • Related parties: Ablecom (CEO is Steve Liang, Charles's brother; Charles and Sara own ~10.5%) made ~95% of Supermicro's chassis in FY26. Compuware (CEO is Bill Liang, another brother) is a contract manufacturer and distributor. In 2018 Liang personally borrowed ~$12.9M from Steve Liang's wife to cover margin calls after the Nasdaq suspension, and repaid it ($16.9M) only on Oct 9, 2025.
  • The rehiring issue: after the $17.5M SEC settlement, Supermicro rehired at least nine people who had resigned over the 2018 scandal, including Wally Liaw: consultant in May 2021, SVP of Business Development in 2022, back on the board in Dec 2023. It also engaged the former CFO as a consultant without telling EY or the audit committee. The special committee called the rehires "reasonable business judgment" with "lapses." In March 2026 the SDNY indicted Liaw, a Taiwan sales manager, and a contractor for allegedly routing ~$2.5B of US-assembled servers through a Southeast Asian buyer, with ≥$510M diverted to China in Apr–May 2025 using forged documents and dummy servers. Liaw pleaded not guilty, and his trial moved from Nov 2, 2026 to Mar 2027. The company is not charged. Its independent probe (Munger Tolles plus AlixPartners, completed Aug 20, 2026) found no evidence that current senior management knew. DOJ (grand jury), BIS export-enforcement, and SEC (new subpoena Apr 28, 2026) inquiries remain open. The company says it has not been told it is a target.
  • Controls: BDO gave an adverse opinion on internal controls for FY24, FY25, and FY26. Three FY25 weaknesses were fixed, and a new one appeared (IT user-access controls). Financial statements got clean (unqualified) opinions.
  • Litigation: consolidated securities class actions from 2024 (motion to dismiss filed Nov 2025, pending), a new consolidated class action from 2026 over the export allegations, and federal and state derivative suits.

Leadership read: Liang's engineering passion and alignment are genuine. The weak spot is the "tone at the top" on controls and people: the people he trusts, the slow CFO change, and family-linked suppliers. Governance has improved on paper (BDO, GC, lead independent director, an ex-Deloitte director) but keeps lagging the company's size.

4. Passion vs "song and dance"

Real passion and substance: - 33 years founder-led, through two crises. - A $1 salary, with pay tied to stock price. - Years of DLC investment before it was fashionable. - US manufacturing long before tariffs made it popular. - First-to-market shipments on new NVIDIA platforms, repeatedly verified (GB300 volume, Vera Rubin shipping). - 3,500 R&D staff out of ~7,000. - Built xAI's Colossus in 122 days, a widely reported and real deployment.

Song-and-dance signals: - Headline order numbers that the company itself says are not firm commitments: "$39B of orders" (June) and "$60B+ new orders" (July). - A capital raise announced the same day as the order headline. - Repeated revenue targets missed or pushed out as "timing" or "customer readiness": - FY25 guide went $26–30B → $23.5–25B → $21.8–22.6B, and it landed at $22.0B. - Q1 FY26 guide went $6–7B → $5.0B. - Q3 FY26 came in at $10.2B vs ~$12.3B consensus. - FY26 came in at $39.1B vs the "at least $40B" floor. - Liang's market-moving X posts. - Promises like "DCBBS will soon contribute significant net income" have not been quantified. - An unfulfilled public pledge to hire a CFO.

Call: the engineering passion is real, not a song and dance. The financial communication has a pattern of optimism that delivery lags. Treat company targets as the ceiling, not the base case.

6. Competition

  • Dell is now larger in AI servers. Its FY27 AI-server revenue guide is $74B with a record ~$95B AI backlog (Sep 1, 2026), and it has a stronger balance sheet, enterprise salesforce, and services. HPE (with Juniper) is smaller in AI systems (~$2.4B of AI orders last quarter) but has strong enterprise and supercomputing (Cray) positions. Lenovo also competes.
  • ODMs: Foxconn (Hon Hai), Quanta, Wistron/Wiwynn, and Inventec build the bulk of hyperscaler GB200/GB300 racks direct at lower margins. That caps Supermicro's share at the top five clouds and pushes it toward neoclouds, sovereigns, xAI-type builders, and enterprise.
  • NVIDIA is both partner and gatekeeper. It controls GPU allocation and increasingly standardizes the rack (MGX/NVL), which compresses integrator value-add.
  • Edge: speed-to-market, DLC breadth, US manufacturing (helps on tariffs and federal TAA), and price. Weak spots: governance discount, balance sheet versus Dell, and concentration.

7. Future / catalysts

  • FY27 guide is $65–72B (+66–84%), with Q1 at $14.5–15.5B. Backlog is "record," but orders can be cancelled or delayed.
  • Vera Rubin ramp through FY27; AMD MI450/Helios; ARM inference platforms.
  • Margin story: enterprise mix, DCBBS software and services, fewer inventory reserves. The company wants "consistent, growing" margins. History says lumpy.
  • Overhangs: a DOJ/BIS/SEC outcome (fines, a monitor, export restrictions, or federal-sales impact are possible), the Liaw trial (Mar 2027), class-action rulings, the CFO hire, and controls remediation.
  • Dilution path: about 130–160M new shares from the preferred by 2029, depending on the stock price (my estimate from $4.3B ÷ ~$27.50–$33). There's also the unused ATM.
  • Macro: the AI capex cycle, HBM/memory and component shortages (cited in the 10-K), tariffs, and power and site readiness at customers (cited for the Q4 slip).

8. Bull case

  1. Revenue scale is real and accelerating: $22B → $39B → guide $65–72B. Even the low end implies ~$4+ EPS at a ~5% net margin (my estimate). That's ~10x for a company growing 60%+.
  2. First-to-market on Vera Rubin plus a DLC lead, at the exact moment rack power density forces liquid cooling.
  3. Diversification is improving: 9 customers above $1B, enterprise and channel at 50% of Q4, US-centric manufacturing, sovereign and neocloud demand.
  4. Funding is done: ~$7.5B cash, and management says the ATM isn't needed.
  5. The independent probe cleared current management on diversion, and the company isn't charged. Clean audit opinions on the financials.
  6. Sentiment and positioning: ~17% of float short, the stock is 25% below its 52-week high, and the consensus is a lukewarm Hold. Upside surprises squeeze hard (Jul 22 +13–20%, Aug 12 +19%).

9. Bear case

  1. Governance is a pattern, not an episode: the 2018 restatement and delisting, the 2020 SEC penalty, the rehiring of nine scandal-era staff, EY's resignation, a co-founder indicted for diversion, three straight adverse controls opinions, a 22-month unfulfilled CFO change, family-linked chassis supply, and open DOJ, BIS, and SEC probes.
  2. Margins and pricing power: ~10–11% gross margin is the base, and Q4's 17.6% was helped by deferring low-margin deals into Q1. Dell and the ODMs compete hard on the same NVIDIA parts.
  3. Concentration: one customer was 28% of the year and ~63% of one quarter. A delay or shift at that customer swings everything (it already caused Q1/Q4 slips).
  4. Cash and dilution: FCF was –$7B. Shares rose 11%, mandatory conversion comes in 2029, and debt is $8.7B. Equity was raised at the lows ($27.50).
  5. Orders aren't revenue: the $39B and $60B numbers are not firm commitments, and guidance has been missed or trimmed repeatedly.
  6. Regulatory tail risk: export-control penalties or federal-sales limits, and possible China-diversion fallout with NVIDIA allocation.
  7. Cycle risk: if AI capex pauses, a $12.9B inventory and a thin margin turn into write-downs.

Bottom line for a holder

You own a real AI infrastructure manufacturer with founder passion, genuine speed and cooling know-how, and a 2027 revenue plan that, if delivered, makes today's price look cheap. You also own a structurally low-margin, cash-hungry, customer-concentrated business whose management has repeatedly over-promised timing, diluted shareholders, and run into accounting, controls, and export-control trouble. The low multiple pays you for that. It isn't a free lunch. Practical stance: keep it a small slice and be willing to trim into spikes (it has run from $19 to $45 in about 6 months). Add only after a clean quarter (revenue at or above the guide midpoint, gross margin ≥10.5%, positive operating cash flow) and a governance positive such as a new CFO or a closed DOJ/SEC matter. Exit triggers: the company becomes a target of the export probe, the largest customer slips again, or another auditor or controls shock.
