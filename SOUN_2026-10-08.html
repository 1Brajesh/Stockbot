Executive summary

Verdict: Speculative Hold / size small. Don't add on the "$500M revenue opportunity" story. Add only after a clean post-LivePerson quarter shows cash burn falling and organic growth you can see. SoundHound is a real voice-AI company. Its three founders have run it for 21 years, it built its own speech stack, and it ships in cars (Stellantis, Hyundai/Kia, Lucid) and in thousands of restaurants. Revenue grew from $21M (2021) to $169M (2025) and is guided to $230–260M in 2026. The problem is everything around that growth:

  • It has never made money. Strip out a non-cash earnout gain and the 2025 loss was ~$177M on $169M of revenue (my calculation from the 10-K). Operating cash burn was $98M in 2025 and $60M in H1 2026.
  • Heavy dilution: about 200M shares at end-2022 became ~487M today (my estimate after the LivePerson deal). That came from ~$657M of at-the-market (ATM) stock sales since 2024, a new $300M ATM, stock paid for acquisitions, and stock comp of ~48% of revenue.
  • Growth is largely bought: SYNQ3 (2024), Amelia (2024), Interactions (2025), and LivePerson (closed Sep 4, 2026), a messaging company whose revenue is shrinking ~12% a year.
  • A credibility gap: it promised profitability "within 2023" and positive operating cash flow by Q4 2023, then adjusted-EBITDA profit by end-2025, and missed all three. It touted a ~$1.2B "cumulative subscriptions and bookings backlog" while the audited contracted backlog (RPO) is $60M.
  • Accounting controls: material weaknesses disclosed for years, earlier periods revised, and a securities class action that partly survived dismissal in May 2026.

The stock is down ~75% from its high and ~40% of the float is sold short. The market is pricing a lot of this already. What's left is a turnaround-and-integration bet, not a proven business.

1. Origins

  • Founded Sep 2005 in the Bay Area as Melodis Corp by three Stanford graduate students: Keyvan Mohajer (CEO), Majid Emami and James Hom. All three are still at the company in 2026.
  • First product: music recognition. Midomi (hum or sing a song to find it) was rebranded SoundHound in 2009, and the company took the SoundHound name in 2010. The app passed 100M users by 2012 and 300M+ downloads (company). It competed with Shazam, which Apple bought in 2018. SoundHound still owns its own music app; it did not sell it to Apple. Today it is immaterial: "monetization" revenue was $55K in Q2 2026. The midomi.com web version shut in Feb 2025 (Wikipedia).
  • Meanwhile it built a general voice assistant tech stack ("Speech-to-Meaning", "Deep Meaning Understanding") and launched Houndify (Dec 2015), a platform that lets other companies add voice to products, plus the consumer Hound assistant app (2016). Hyundai/Kia put SoundHound's music recognition in cars from ~2015.
  • Funding: ~$40M by 2015; a $75M round in Jan 2017 including NVIDIA, Samsung and Kleiner Perkins; unicorn status by Jan 2018; a $100M round led by Tencent (May 2018). Strategic investors also included Hyundai and Daimler/Mercedes.
  • SPAC: merged with Archimedes Tech SPAC Partners (closed Apr 26–27, 2022) at a ~$2.1B valuation and renamed itself "SoundHound AI." The SPAC delivered far less cash than planned. The stock went from ~$7.50 at debut to $0.97 by Dec 2022.
  • 2022–2023 crisis: a 10% layoff (Nov 2022), then ~40–50% of staff cut (Jan 2023). It survived on an equity line, preferred stock and a $100M Atlas Credit term loan (Apr 2023). The Jan 2023 restructuring release promised "profitability within 2023" and positive operating cash flow by Q4 2023. Neither happened (2023 net loss –$88.9M, operating cash flow –$68.3M).
  • 2024 AI mania: NVIDIA's Feb 14, 2024 13F revealed a small leftover stake (~1.7M shares from the 2017 round), and the stock jumped from $2.28 to $3.82 in three days and to $5.86 by Mar 8. The company sold heavily into the rally ($407M of ATM sales in 2024). The stock peaked at a $24.23 close on Dec 26, 2024 (intraday high ~$25).
  • Feb 14, 2025: NVIDIA's 13F showed it had sold its entire stake; SOUN fell ~28% that day ($15.25 → $10.97). The partnership (NVIDIA DRIVE / edge AI work) reportedly continued; the stake was always tiny relative to NVIDIA.
  • Acquisition spree: SYNQ3 restaurant voice ordering (Jan 2024, ~$15.8M fair value), Allset ordering marketplace (Jun 2024, small), Amelia enterprise AI agents (Aug 2024, $98.6M plus $121.5M assumed debt, repaid Dec 2024, plus up to 16.8M earnout shares), Interactions call-center AI (Sep 2025, $76.1M preliminary consideration incl. $41.5M debt repayment, plus up to $25M cash earnout), an unnamed asset purchase (May 2026, up to $30.2M cash), and LivePerson (closed Sep 4, 2026).

Skeptical read: this is a genuine 21-year technology story with a lot of survival grit. But it has pivoted many times (music app → consumer assistant → licensing → restaurants → enterprise call-center agents → omnichannel customer-service platform). It has been an expert at raising money when its stock is hot.

3. Leadership quality

  • Dr. Keyvan Mohajer (co-founder, CEO since 2005; Stanford PhD in electrical engineering per company bio). A true technologist-founder and evangelist. Pay: $565K salary, 2025 summary compensation $9.3M (mostly stock), a 2025 bonus target of $546K that the CEO and CFO recommended be paid at 0%, and a Jul 31, 2026 grant of 1.5M RSUs plus 375K performance units that vest only if the stock hits price hurdles by Jul 2029.
  • Dr. Majid Emami (co-founder, executive): holds 51% of Class B, about 23% of votes, more than the CEO.
  • James Hom (co-founder, Chief Product Officer, director): also served as interim CFO Apr–Sep 2026.
  • CFO turnover: Nitesh Sharan (CFO through the SPAC and growth years) resigned effective Apr 3, 2026 to become CFO of Quantinuum, about 2.5 weeks before the LivePerson deal was announced (Apr 21). Hom filled in. John Collins, LivePerson's CFO/COO (and interim CEO 2023–24), became SoundHound CFO at the deal's close (Sep 4, 2026). Positive: he is a cost-cutter who led LivePerson's swing from ~$100M+ cash burn to positive free cash flow and its debt restructurings (company). Skeptical: he comes from a company whose revenue and stock collapsed on his watch.
  • Michael Zagorsek, COO (long-time exec).
  • Board (5): Mohajer, Hom, plus three independents: Larry Marcus (Walden Venture Capital, an early investor), Diana Sroka, Dr. Eric Ball. That is small for a ~$2.7B company that just doubled in size, and two of five seats are founders.
  • Voting control: dual-class. Class B carries 10 votes per share. At Mar 30, 2026 founders held all 32.5M Class B shares and ~46% of total votes (Mohajer 19.8%, Emami 23.1%, Hom 2.6%), with ~9% of the economics. After LivePerson dilution, my estimate is ~42% of votes. They can block almost anything; outside shareholders can't force change.
  • Insider ownership (economic): Mohajer ~14.1M Class B plus ~3.7M Class A (incl. unvested awards, Form 4 Sep 2026), so ~3.7% of the company, worth ~$98M at $5.52. Meaningful skin, but not owner-operator-level.
  • Insider selling: a scan of Form 4s since Oct 2025 shows regular quarterly sales by Mohajer, Emami, Hom, Zagorsek and others (e.g., Mohajer 137,290 shares at $6.33 on Sep 15, 2026, ≈$0.87M). The filings say these were sold to cover tax withholding on vesting stock, not discretionary dumping. I found no open-market insider purchases. Capybara alleged ~7M insider shares were sold into the Feb–Mar 2024 spike (unverified).
  • Controls and accounting: the FY2025 10-K (auditor PwC, since 2023, after Armanino exited) reports material weaknesses in the control environment, risk assessment, accounting for complex transactions, and segregation of duties. These caused revisions of 2022–2023 quarterly statements plus immaterial errors in 2023–2025. Disclosure controls were still "not effective" at Jun 30, 2026. The 2024 10-K was filed late (Mar 2025) amid acquisition-accounting issues.
  • Litigation: Liles v. SoundHound (securities class action, class period Mar 1, 2024 – Mar 11, 2025, names Mohajer and Sharan). On May 19, 2026 the judge dismissed the Amelia-accounting claims but let some internal-control claims proceed; mediation was set for Aug 25, 2026 (outcome not yet disclosed). Also VB Assets v. SoundHound (patent suit over former VoiceBox patents, Delaware, filed Nov 2024).

Leadership read: the founders are clearly committed. They've stuck through near-death, pay themselves modestly in cash, and passed on 2025 bonuses. The weak spots are a long-running controls problem, a CFO seat that turned over twice in five months, a tiny founder-controlled board, and a habit of financing the company through the stock.

4. Passion vs "song and dance"

Real passion and substance:

  • 21 years with the same three founders, through a 2022 crash to $0.97 and a 50% layoff.
  • Built its own speech recognition and language engine years before ChatGPT. Supports dozens of languages (25 completed by Jan 2023, company).
  • Real, verifiable deployments: Stellantis/Jeep/DS cars, Lucid, the Kia Sorento in India, and public restaurant rollouts (White Castle, Jersey Mike's, Red Lobster, Church's, Torchy's).
  • Leadership waived its 2025 cash bonuses even though revenue targets were hit.
  • Revenue guidance has generally been met: the 2025 guide was met at $168.9M, and 2026 was raised in August.

Song-and-dance signals:

  • Broken profitability promises: "profitability within 2023" and "operating cash flow positive by Q4" (Jan 5, 2023 press release). Actual 2023: net loss –$88.9M, operating cash flow –$68.3M. Then "adjusted EBITDA profitable by end of 2025": Q4 2025 came in at –$7.4M, and Q1 2026 fell back to –$26.7M. No new dated profitability target has been given since.
  • Backlog inflation: ">$300M cumulative bookings backlog" (2023) grew to "nearly $1.2B cumulative subscriptions and bookings backlog" (Q4 2024). That metric includes modeled future subscriptions. Audited remaining performance obligations (RPO) were $79.5M (Dec 2025) and $60.0M (Jun 2026).
  • Superlatives: OASYS is "the world's first self-learning orchestrated agentic AI platform," "category-defining," "gold standard." Press releases lean on unnamed "7-figure" and "8-figure" deals.
  • Hype riding: the rename to "SoundHound AI" at the SPAC, and heavy ATM selling into the 2024 NVIDIA-13F spike. The NVIDIA stake was a 2017 venture leftover, not a new endorsement.
  • "Record" quarters that lean on acquisitions and one-offs: Q2 2026's +40% sequential jump was driven by point-in-time license revenue ($20.5M vs ~$7.2M in Q1). The 10-Q credits "certain new, higher margin license deals," and the subscription growth was "mainly driven by revenue from acquisitions."
  • LivePerson framing: the company says the combination is a "$500M revenue opportunity from existing customers." Yet the 2025 pro forma revenue of the three pieces (SoundHound + Interactions + LivePerson) was already $455.4M (company pro forma, Jul 2026), and Q1 2026 pro forma was $101.2M (~$405M annualized). The 2027 "at minimum" $350–400M outlook is below what the pieces already generate. That implies a big LivePerson shrink (management cites "at least $100M of growable" LivePerson revenue), or a conservative guide. Either way, "$500M" is roughly where the parts already were, not new growth.

Call: the founders' passion for voice AI is genuine. This is not a fake company. But the financial storytelling has repeatedly run ahead of reality. Treat company targets as the best case, and judge it only on GAAP cash flow and per-share numbers.

7. Competition
