The 14-Step Stock Selection Process (Summary)

Tom Nash. Full text, published as written.

🦴 Caveman version

Same 14 steps, short version. Checklist before you buy any stock.

Know your rules. Sales growing and making money. One-sentence explanation. Big market. Know revenue sources. 5+ years of good history. Strong balance sheet (debt vs cash). Moat. Great management. Write the hate case. Value two ways. Check bad scenario still OK. Decide size first. Re-check every quarter.

Be methodical. No hype. Buy good companies. It is discipline, not gambling.

1. Define your investing constitution

Decide who you are as an investor before buying anything:

  • Long-term vs short-term
  • Type of stocks you buy
  • Goals, rules, scoring system

You don’t invest without a clear game plan.

2. Do a basic sanity check

Confirm the company:

  • Has revenue growth
  • Has profits
  • Has real fundamentals
  • Never buy a stock based only on a “blue-sky story.”

3. Explain the business in one sentence

If you cannot clearly explain:

  • What the company does
  • How it makes money

👉 You do not buy the stock.

4. Understand the total addressable market (TAM)

Determine:

  • How big the market really is
  • Whether the product is niche or scalable

No growth runway = no investment.

5. Understand all revenue engines

Know exactly where revenue comes from:

  • Products
  • Services
  • Segments

If you don’t understand it, you don’t invest.

6. Review financial history (5+ years)

Study:

  • Revenue trends
  • Profitability trends
  • Margin trends

You’re looking for consistency and improvement.

7. Analyze the balance sheet (loan officer mindset)

Treat it like a bank loan:

  • How much debt they owe
  • How much cash they have
  • What assets or collateral exist

Balance sheet strength matters.

8. Assess the moat

Identify defenses against competition:

  • Patents
  • Network effects
  • Customer lock-in
  • Brand or scale advantages

No moat = higher long-term risk.

9. Evaluate management quality

Only invest if:

  • Management is elite
  • Capital allocation is smart
  • Execution track record is strong

Bad management kills good businesses.

10. Write a full bear case

Pretend you hate the company:

  • List all risks
  • Write the strongest possible bearish thesis

If you still want the stock afterward, proceed.

11. Run valuation using at least two models

Use multiple approaches:

  • DCF
  • Multiples (P/E, EV/EBIT, P/S, etc.)

Never rely on just one valuation method.

12. Build scenarios (bear / base / bull)

Ask:

  • Would I still want this stock in the bear case?

Only invest if the bear case is acceptable.

13. Decide portfolio sizing

Determine in advance:

  • How much goes into this stock

How it fits with S&P 500, bonds, cash, etc.

Position size is part of risk control.

14. Re-evaluate every quarter

Every quarter:

  • Recheck fundamentals
  • Recheck thesis
  • Confirm it still meets your criteria

Investing is ongoing monitoring, not set-and-forget.

🧠 Core Philosophy Behind the Process

  • Be methodical
  • Avoid hype, memes, and trends
  • Buy good companies
  • Use DCA, buy more when fundamentals are strong and price is down
  • Treat investing as a discipline, not gambling
